Phase 1: Mixbus 12 Pro Import, Prep & Gain Staging
0/7
Stem Integrity Check: Confirm all stems start at the same point, share the same length and the project sample rate, and aren't clipped. Make sure timing, tuning, and comping are final so no further editing is needed after this point.
Session Organization & Track Grouping: Color-code and organize imported stems: Drums, Bass, Guitars (Rhythm L/R, Lead/Ambient L/R), Vocal 1 (Clean), Vocal 2 (Screaming), and Effects/Auxes.
Clean Editing & Crossfades: Apply crossfades (≥ 10 ms) on all spliced audio clips to prevent pops. Trim clip starts 1 to 2 bars before the first hit and trim unused noise gaps across high-gain guitar takes and vocal pauses.
Channel Strip De-Noising: Engage Mixbus 12 Pro’s built-in per-channel DeNoiser on high-gain guitar tracks, mic bleed channels, and vocal takes to eliminate background hum before compression.
Phase Alignment: Check phase correlation between drum multi-mics (top/bottom snare, kick in/out) and multi-mic bass signals (DI vs. Amp mic). Flip channel phase switches in Mixbus to ensure maximum low-end impact.
Sub-Group Routing (Mixbusses): Route individual channels to dedicated Mixbus sub-groups:
  • Bus 1: Drums (Kick, Snare, Toms, Cymbals, Rooms)
  • Bus 2: Bass (Clean DI + Distorted Upper-Mid Track)
  • Bus 3: Guitars (Rhythm Guitars 1 & 2, Lead/Ambient Guitars)
  • Bus 4: Vocal 1 (Clean Lead & Harmonies)
  • Bus 5: Vocal 2 (Screaming / Harsh Lead)
Gain Staging (0 dBVU / -18 dBFS Sweet Spot): Use the input Trim knob at the top of each Mixbus channel strip to set the average level near 0 VU (about -18 dBFS) on the strip meter, keeping peaks well below 0 dBFS, to hit the analog console modeling sweet spot before adding processing.
Reference Track Setup: Import 1 to 2 commercial post-hardcore reference tracks. Assign them directly to the Mixbus Monitor Strip / Audition Path (bypassing the Master Bus and console saturation) and level-match them to your unmastered mix by loudness (Mixbus Loudness Analyzer) or by ear, rather than a fixed dB offset.
Phase 2: Static Volume Balance, Panning & Real-Time Balance Tests
0/4
Fader Balancing (Conversation Level): Set monitoring volume to a quiet conversation level. Build the static mix fader balance around the kick drum, snare, bass, and the two lead vocals.
Post-Hardcore Stereo Placement (50/50 & LCR Method):
  • Center (0%): Kick Drum, Snare Drum, Bass DI, Lead Vocal 1 (Clean Chorus/Verses), Lead Vocal 2 (Main Screams).
  • Hard Left / Hard Right (100% L/R): Rhythm Guitar 1 (Left) and Rhythm Guitar 2 (Right) for a thick wall-of-sound rhythm section.
  • Medium Wide (50% L/R): Lead/Ambient Guitars counter-panned against rhythm lines; Cymbal Overheads, Tom pans, and Vocal 1 double/harmonies.
  • Vocal Separation: Micro-pan Lead Vocal 1 (Clean) 3–5% left and Lead Vocal 2 (Scream) 3–5% right during dual-vocal sections to keep them distinct in the center image.
[STRESS TEST] The Mono Summing Stress Test

Hit the MONO button on your Mixbus monitor section regularly.
Verification: Ensure center elements (lead vocals, snare crack, kick drum) do not disappear due to phase cancellation or frequency masking. Adjust EQ or levels in mono until clear before returning to stereo.

[STRESS TEST] The Fletcher-Munson "Whisper" Test

Drop monitor volume to near-silence (a faint whisper).
Verification: Confirm that only key focal elements remain clearly audible (kick click, snare crack, lead vocals). If rhythm guitars or background FX dominate at whisper volume, reduce their faders.

Phase 3: Dynamic Control, Compression & Stress Auditing
0/7
Drums: Apply channel compression (Harrison 32C / SSL 9000J strip) to snare and kick with a slow attack (20–40 ms) for punch and fast release for sustain. Send drum tracks to a parallel drum compression bus (crushed with fast attack/release) and blend under the main drum bus for heavy post-hardcore energy.
Bass Guitar: Split bass processing into two paths or use parallel routing: heavy opto compression on the clean low end for smooth sub-bass, and fast VCA compression/saturation on the upper-mid grid for pick articulation.
Rhythm & Lead Guitars: Keep rhythm guitar compression minimal (≤ 1.5–2 dB) since high-gain amplifiers already compress dynamics. Use bus compression on the Guitar Mixbus to glue rhythm and lead guitars together.
Lead Vocal 1 (Clean): Apply 3–6 dB of gain reduction using a two-stage compressor approach (fast peak-catching FET followed by an Opto leveler for smooth sustain). Engage the channel DeEsser as needed to control sibilance.
Lead Vocal 2 (Screams): Use fast VCA or FET compression to handle extreme dynamic spikes. Engage Mixbus 12 Pro’s built-in channel DeEsser (taming sibilance and harshness between 5 kHz and 7 kHz).
Gating & Expansion: Use the channel Gate/Expander on drum bleed (especially toms) and high-gain guitars to tighten stop-start riffs and clean the noise between chugs. Set release by ear so decays and palm-mute tails aren't chopped.
[STRESS TEST] Level-Matched Plugin Bypass (The "Better vs. Louder" Trap)

Match output gain on every compressor/EQ plugin so the signal is volume-matched when bypassed.
Verification: Rapidly toggle plugins on and off to confirm processing genuinely improves tone and dynamic stability rather than just making the audio louder.

[STRESS TEST] The Master Compressor "Magnifying Glass"

Temporarily insert a master compressor pulling a severe 10–15 dB of gain reduction with a fast release.
Verification: Listen for severe pumping or choking. If the kick drum crushes the mix, sub-bass (20–50 Hz) is uncontrolled. If a vocal word or guitar note causes choking, locate and notch that specific resonance spike on its track. Disable the compressor afterward.

Phase 4: Tone Shaping, Surgical EQ & Band Auditing
0/7
Sub-Bass (20 Hz–60 Hz): High-pass non-bass instruments. Solo sub-bass to ensure only kick drum sub-harmonics and bass fundamentals live here.
Bass & Low-End (60 Hz–200 Hz): Carve a slight notch on the bass guitar around the kick drum’s fundamental frequency (e.g., 60 Hz or 80 Hz) to create separation.
Low Mids (200 Hz–600 Hz): Cut mud accumulation on high-gain guitars (250 Hz–400 Hz) and Clean/Scream vocals (300 Hz–500 Hz) using narrow parametric notches.
Midrange (600 Hz–2 kHz): Clear space for Lead Vocal 1 (clean body around 800 Hz–1.2 kHz) and Lead Vocal 2 (screaming resonance around 1.5 kHz–2 kHz) by applying slight dynamic or static dips in the rhythm guitars.
Upper Mids (2 kHz–8 kHz): Boost kick drum point/beater click (3 kHz–5 kHz) and snare crack (3 kHz–4 kHz). Boost aggression on Vocal 2 (Screams) around 3.5 kHz–5 kHz. Notch out harsh, whistle-like resonances in high-gain guitars (3 kHz–4.5 kHz) using TDR Nova or Mixbus channel EQ.
Highs & Air (8 kHz+): Apply a gentle high shelf boost on Vocal 1 (Clean) and Cymbal Overheads for air, ensuring high-pass safety cuts prevent buildup.
[STRESS TEST] Band-Pass Frequency Auditing

Insert a narrow band-pass filter on your master monitor path to audit critical problem zones.
Verification (200 Hz–500 Hz): Confirm this band sounds distinct and not like an illegible wall of mud.
Verification (3 kHz–5 kHz): Check for harsh, ear-fatiguing whistle resonances from cymbals or amp cabinet models and notch them out on individual tracks.

Phase 5: Spatial Depth, Saturation & Reference Flicking
0/6
Mixbus Console Saturation: Drive the built-in Mixbus Tape Saturation knobs on the Drum, Bass, and Guitar mixbusses (1–2 dB of soft drive) to add glue and harmonic warmth.
Ambience & Room Reverb: Set up an Aux track with a short room reverb (0.8–1.2s decay). Send drums and rhythm guitars to glue them into a single physical room space.
Vocal Depth (Clean Vocal 1): Set up a plate or hall reverb aux for Clean Vocal 1 to add length in ambient sections, ducked slightly by the dry vocal using sidechain compression.
Vocal Delay (Screaming Vocal 2 & Guitars): Use a stereo slapback delay (30–80 ms) or timed 1/4-note delay aux on Vocal 2 and lead guitar solos/arpeggios to build width without pushing them back in the mix.
Special Effects & Modulation: Apply chorus or phaser effects on intro/bridge ambient guitars or clean vocal drops for tonal variety.
[STRESS TEST] Level-Matched Reference Flicking

Map a single key command to toggle instantly between your mix and the level-matched reference track.
Verification: Flick back and forth every 10–15 minutes for 5 seconds at a time to immediately spot tonal drift (e.g., missing low end, dull highs, or recessed midrange).

Phase 6: Dynamic Volume Automation
0/3
Section Automation: Automate overall bus levels or faders to increase track volume by +0.5 dB to +1.0 dB heading into heavy post-hardcore choruses or breakdowns.
Dual Lead Vocal Rides: Automate fader levels word-by-word on Vocal 1 and Vocal 2 so every scream, whisper, and clean line remains legible over dense guitar walls.
Instrument Call-and-Response: Automate lead guitar fills, snare roll transitions, or bass slides upward during vocal pauses.
Phase 7: Pre-Mastering Quality Control & Safety Checks
0/8
Peak Headroom & Metering:
  • True Peak: Ensure peaks sit between -3.0 dBTP and -6.0 dBTP with no digital clipping (0 dBFS).
  • Loudness (reference only): Glance at the Mixbus Loudness Analyzer to confirm nothing is wildly off, but don't chase a loudness target. Final loudness is the mastering engineer's call.
Bus Processing & Limiting Check: Confirm brickwall limiters or clipping plugins on the master bus are turned OFF. Limit gain reduction on the master bus (Harrison Leveler / SSL Compressor) to a maximum of 1.5 dB to 2 dB on peak passages. Remove master volume fader fade-ins/outs so the mastering engineer controls track tails.
Phase & Frequency Safety: Stereo Phase Correlation meter must stay between +0.5 and +1.0 during heavy bass and kick sections. Engage HPFs (80 Hz to 120 Hz) on non-bass tracks (guitars, vocals, room mics). Ensure DC offset filtering is active on render.
Frequency Bracketing & Band Soloing: Solo Sub (20–60 Hz) to confirm no rumble, Bass/Mud (60–250 Hz) for separation, Midrange (250 Hz–2 kHz) for vocal cut, Upper Mids (2–6 kHz) for snare attack and bite, and Highs (6 kHz+) to confirm no masking.
Objective A/B Referencing: Level-match the commercial post-hardcore reference track to your mix by loudness (Mixbus Loudness Analyzer) or by ear. Route reference track directly to monitor section (bypassing master bus saturation/compression). Compare band-by-band sub, mid, and high balances using Voxengo SPAN or TDR Nova.
Visual Tonal Balance Analysis: Confirm overall frequency spectrum follows a smooth downward curve (~-4.5 dB per octave). Check for narrow, harsh spikes in high-gain guitars or cymbals.
Translation & Mono Checks: Sum output to mono via Mixbus Monitor Section; ensure kick, snare, and lead vocals stay centered without phase cancellation. Press DIM knob / lower monitoring volume to near-silent levels; verify lead vocals, snare crack, and kick click remain audible.
Track Tail & Noise Cleanup: Start point set 1–2 bars before sound. End point allows final decays/reverbs to fade completely to silence. Gaps and unused sections muted or gated.
Save Final Snapshot: Save a named session snapshot (e.g., Final_Mix_v1) right before exporting so you can return to this exact mix for any revisions.
Outside-the-Room Listening: Take a break for fresh ears, then listen through once on headphones and on phone or car speakers. Note and fix anything that translates badly.

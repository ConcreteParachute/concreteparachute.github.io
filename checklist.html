The Ultimate Stop Rule

Don't use "I can still hear it" as your indicator that you're good to keep mixing. Your ears adapt surprisingly quickly. If you find yourself repeatedly making a change, undoing it, then making essentially the same change again, stop mixing and take a break.

Daily Mix Schedule

Work in 50-minute blocks, followed by a 15-minute break where you genuinely stop listening to music. After 3 blocks, take a 45-minute break.

When returning from a break, play the entire song from the beginning at a moderate level before touching anything. Give your brain a chance to re-establish the song as a whole.

The 5-Pass Approach

Revisit an important mix 3–5 separate times rather than attempting a marathon session.

  • Pass 1 — Build Get balances, panning, basic EQ/compression, effects and overall direction working.
  • Pass 2 — Fresh Ears Come back after several hours or next day. Fix the obvious stuff you couldn't hear anymore.
  • Pass 3 — Detail Automation, vocal consistency, transitions, masking, low-end relationships, effects.
  • Pass 4 — Translation Listen on headphones, car, small speakers. Take notes rather than fixing immediately.
  • Pass 5 — Final Make only the changes that survived the previous checks.

Phase 1: Mixbus 12 Pro Import, Prep & Gain Staging
0/8
Stem Integrity Check: Confirm all stems start at the same point, share the same length and the project sample rate, and aren't clipped. Make sure timing, tuning, and comping are final so no further editing is needed after this point.
Session Organization & Track Grouping: Color-code and organize imported stems: Drums, Bass, Guitars (Rhythm L/R, Lead/Ambient L/R), Vocal 1 (Clean), Vocal 2 (Screaming), and Effects/Auxes.
Clean Editing & Crossfades: Apply crossfades (≥ 10 ms) on all spliced audio clips to prevent pops. Trim clip starts 1 to 2 bars before the first hit and trim unused noise gaps across high-gain guitar takes and vocal pauses.
Channel Strip De-Noising: Engage Mixbus 12 Pro’s built-in per-channel DeNoiser on high-gain guitar tracks, mic bleed channels, and vocal takes to eliminate background hum before compression.
Phase Alignment: Check phase correlation between drum multi-mics (top/bottom snare, kick in/out) and multi-mic bass signals (DI vs. Amp mic). Flip channel phase switches in Mixbus to ensure maximum low-end impact.
Sub-Group Routing (Mixbusses): Route individual channels to dedicated Mixbus sub-groups:
  • Bus 1: Drums (Kick, Snare, Toms, Cymbals, Rooms)
  • Bus 2: Bass (Clean DI + Distorted Upper-Mid Track)
  • Bus 3: Guitars (Rhythm Guitars 1 & 2, Lead/Ambient Guitars)
  • Bus 4: Vocal 1 (Clean Lead & Harmonies)
  • Bus 5: Vocal 2 (Screaming / Harsh Lead)
Gain Staging (0 dBVU / -18 dBFS Sweet Spot): Use the input Trim knob at the top of each Mixbus channel strip to set the average level near 0 VU (about -18 dBFS) on the strip meter, keeping peaks well below 0 dBFS, to hit the analog console modeling sweet spot before adding processing.
Reference Track Setup: Import 1 to 2 commercial post-hardcore reference tracks. Assign them directly to the Mixbus Monitor Strip / Audition Path and level-match them to your unmastered mix by loudness.
Phase 2: Static Volume Balance, Panning & Reference Flicking
0/5
Fader Balancing (Conversation Level): Set monitoring volume to a quiet conversation level. Build the static mix fader balance around the kick drum, snare, bass, and the two lead vocals.
Rule: Fader before plugin. If a simple fader level fixes the balance, do not reach for an EQ.
Post-Hardcore Stereo Placement (50/50 & LCR Method):
  • Center (0%): Kick, Snare, Bass DI, Lead Vocals.
  • Hard L/R (100%): Rhythm Guitars for a thick wall-of-sound.
  • Medium Wide (50% L/R): Lead/Ambient Guitars, Overheads, Toms.
  • Vocal Separation: Micro-pan Lead 1 (Clean) 3–5% left and Lead 2 (Scream) 3–5% right during dual-vocal sections.
[STRESS TEST] Level-Matched Reference Flicking

Map a single key command to toggle instantly between your mix and the level-matched reference track.
Rule: Reference before memory. Regularly compare to level-matched references instead of trusting your ears' adaptation. Flick back and forth every 10–15 minutes for 5 seconds to spot tonal drift.

[STRESS TEST] The Mono Summing Stress Test

Hit the MONO button on your Mixbus monitor section regularly. Ensure center elements don't disappear to phase cancellation before returning to stereo.

[STRESS TEST] The Fletcher-Munson "Whisper" Test

Drop monitor volume to near-silence. Confirm only key focal elements remain audible (kick click, snare crack, vocals). If guitars dominate at whisper volume, reduce faders.

Phase 3: Dynamic Control, Compression & Stress Auditing
0/7
Rule: Problem before processor.

Never EQ, compress, or add reverb simply because the checklist says you can. Only apply these processors if the track genuinely presents a dynamic or frequency problem that needs solving. Checking an item off means you considered it, not that you had to use it.

Drums: Apply channel compression to snare and kick with a slow attack (20–40 ms) for punch. Send drum tracks to a parallel drum compression bus (crushed with fast attack/release) and blend under the main drum bus.
Bass Guitar: Split bass processing into two paths or use parallel routing: heavy opto compression on the clean low end for smooth sub-bass, and fast VCA compression/saturation on the upper-mid grid for pick articulation.
Rhythm & Lead Guitars: Keep rhythm guitar compression minimal (≤ 1.5–2 dB) since high-gain amplifiers already compress dynamics. Use bus compression on the Guitar Mixbus to glue rhythm and lead guitars together.
Lead Vocal 1 (Clean): Apply 3–6 dB of gain reduction using a two-stage compressor approach (fast peak-catching FET followed by an Opto leveler for smooth sustain).
Lead Vocal 2 (Screams): Use fast VCA or FET compression to handle extreme dynamic spikes. Engage built-in channel DeEsser (taming sibilance between 5–7 kHz).
Gating & Expansion: Use the channel Gate/Expander on drum bleed and high-gain guitars to tighten stop-start riffs and clean the noise between chugs. Set release by ear so decays aren't chopped.
[STRESS TEST] Level-Matched Plugin Bypass

Match output gain on every compressor/EQ plugin so the signal is volume-matched when bypassed. Rapidly toggle plugins on and off to confirm processing genuinely improves tone rather than just making it louder.

Phase 4: Tone Shaping, Surgical EQ & Band Auditing
0/7

Rule: Problem before processor. Checking an item off means you considered it, not that you had to use it.

Sub-Bass (20 Hz–60 Hz): High-pass non-bass instruments. Solo sub-bass to ensure only kick drum sub-harmonics and bass fundamentals live here.
Bass & Low-End (60 Hz–200 Hz): Carve a slight notch on the bass guitar around the kick drum’s fundamental frequency to create separation.
Low Mids (200 Hz–600 Hz): Cut mud accumulation on high-gain guitars and vocals using narrow parametric notches.
Midrange (600 Hz–2 kHz): Clear space for Lead Vocal 1 (clean body around 800 Hz–1.2 kHz) and Lead Vocal 2 (screaming resonance around 1.5 kHz–2 kHz) by applying slight dips in rhythm guitars.
Upper Mids (2 kHz–8 kHz): Boost kick drum point and snare crack. Notch out harsh, whistle-like resonances in high-gain guitars (3 kHz–4.5 kHz).
Highs & Air (8 kHz+): Apply a gentle high shelf boost on Vocal 1 (Clean) and Cymbal Overheads for air, ensuring high-pass safety cuts prevent buildup.
[STRESS TEST] Band-Pass Frequency Auditing

Insert a narrow band-pass filter on your master monitor path to audit critical problem zones. Confirm 200–500 Hz sounds distinct (not a wall of mud) and 3–5 kHz has no ear-fatiguing whistles. Remove the filter afterward.

Phase 5: Spatial Depth & Saturation
0/5

Rule: Problem before processor. Checking an item off means you considered it, not that you had to use it.

Mixbus Console Saturation: Drive the built-in Mixbus Tape Saturation knobs on the Drum, Bass, and Guitar mixbusses (1–2 dB of soft drive) to add glue and harmonic warmth.
Ambience & Room Reverb: Set up an Aux track with a short room reverb (0.8–1.2s decay). Send drums and rhythm guitars to glue them into a single physical room space.
Vocal Depth (Clean Vocal 1): Set up a plate or hall reverb aux for Clean Vocal 1 to add length in ambient sections, ducked slightly by the dry vocal using sidechain compression.
Vocal Delay (Screaming Vocal 2 & Guitars): Use a stereo slapback delay (30–80 ms) or timed 1/4-note delay aux on Vocal 2 and lead guitar solos/arpeggios to build width without pushing them back in the mix.
Special Effects & Modulation: Apply chorus or phaser effects on intro/bridge ambient guitars or clean vocal drops for tonal variety.
Phase 6: Dynamic Volume Automation
0/3
Section Automation: Automate overall bus levels or faders to increase track volume by +0.5 dB to +1.0 dB heading into heavy post-hardcore choruses or breakdowns.
Rule: Mix the entire song. Don't confuse a perfect 8-bar loop with a finished mix. Ensure transitions flow naturally from start to finish.
Dual Lead Vocal Rides: Automate fader levels word-by-word on Vocal 1 and Vocal 2 so every scream, whisper, and clean line remains legible over dense guitar walls.
Instrument Call-and-Response: Automate lead guitar fills, snare roll transitions, or bass slides upward during vocal pauses.
Phase 7: Pre-Mastering Quality Control & Safety Checks
0/8
Peak Headroom & Metering: Ensure peaks sit between -3.0 dBTP and -6.0 dBTP with no digital clipping. Glance at loudness analyzers but do not chase targets.
Bus Processing & Limiting Check: Confirm brickwall limiters or clipping plugins on the master bus are turned OFF.
Phase & Frequency Safety: Keep the Phase Correlation meter mostly positive with no sustained negative readings. Bass and kick should sit near +1; wide guitar sections will read lower, which is fine if the mono check still holds up.
Frequency Bracketing & Band Soloing: Solo bands (Sub, Bass, Mid, Upper Mids, Highs) individually to confirm separation and lack of masking.
Visual Tonal Balance Analysis: Confirm overall frequency spectrum follows a smooth downward curve using an analyzer.
Track Tail & Noise Cleanup: Ensure start point is 1–2 bars before sound, and end point allows final decays/reverbs to fade completely to silence.
Save Final Snapshot: Save a named session snapshot right before exporting so you can return to this exact mix for any revisions.
Outside-the-Room Listening: Take a break, then listen once on headphones and car speakers to note translations issues.
Rule: Fresh ears get the final vote. Do this final listen after a meaningful break or the next morning before making any further adjustments.

0/4
